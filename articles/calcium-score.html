Understanding Your Calcium Score

A coronary calcium score can help personalize prevention. It is one part of your risk assessment.

Updated September 8, 2026

Start here

A coronary calcium score can help personalize prevention. It is one part of your risk assessment.

What the scan measures

A coronary artery calcium (CAC) scan is a noncontrast CT scan that measures calcified plaque in the heart arteries. It uses a small amount of radiation. The result is usually reported as an Agatston score, sometimes with a percentile for people of similar age and sex.

A calcium score does not measure the percentage of narrowing in an artery. It also does not show all noncalcified plaque.

Making sense of your result

General meaning of a CAC result
ScoreWhat it tells us
0No calcified plaque detected. Often reassuring for near-term risk, but it does not exclude all plaque or explain away symptoms.
1-99Calcified plaque is present. Discuss prevention in the context of your age and other risk factors.
100-299A larger plaque burden that strengthens the case for intensive risk-factor management.
300 or higherA substantial plaque burden. Make a clear prevention and follow-up plan with your clinician.

What might change afterward?

The result may help settle an uncertain decision about cholesterol-lowering treatment. Current guidance uses overall cardiovascular risk and the amount of calcium to inform LDL goals. A zero score does not automatically remove the need for treatment, especially with conditions such as diabetes, very high LDL or a strong family history.

Do not start aspirin because of the score alone. Its possible benefit must be weighed against bleeding risk, age and overall cardiovascular risk. If aspirin is already prescribed, discuss changes with the prescriber.

Who needs a different test?

CAC is mainly a prevention tool for selected people without symptoms. Chest symptoms may require a different assessment, such as a stress test or coronary CTA, depending on the situation.

Repeating CAC is not a routine way to measure whether a statin is working. Your clinician can explain whether another scan would change care. Ask the imaging center about cost and coverage before scheduling.

When symptoms change the plan

New severe chest discomfort, trouble breathing, fainting or stroke symptoms warrant emergency evaluation. Call 911; do not wait for a screening scan or a routine results visit. Discuss new, less urgent symptoms with your clinician even if your calcium score was zero.
