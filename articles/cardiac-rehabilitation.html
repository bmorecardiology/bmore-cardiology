Cardiac Rehabilitation

Cardiac rehab combines supervised activity, education and support to help you recover and build confidence.

Updated September 8, 2026

Start here

Cardiac rehab combines supervised activity, education and support to help you recover and build confidence.

What happens in cardiac rehab?

A cardiac rehabilitation program helps you develop a safe activity routine, understand your medicines and risk factors, and work on food choices, tobacco cessation and emotional well-being. It is more than an exercise class.

Your team assesses your needs and sets goals with you. Sessions may involve monitoring and individualized exercise progression as well as practical education.

Who may be eligible?

People recovering from a heart attack, stent procedure, bypass surgery or certain valve procedures may qualify. Some people with stable heart failure also qualify. Clinical suitability and insurance coverage are separate questions; confirm both with the program and your care team.

Making it work in real life

Ask your clinician for a referral and whom to call to schedule. Discuss transportation, work hours, cost and caregiving needs. If attending in person is difficult, ask about available home-based or hybrid options that are appropriate for you.

Bring your medication list and questions to the first visit. Tell the team about new symptoms, medication changes and what you want to be able to do again.

What happens after the program?

Work with the team on a sustainable activity and prevention plan for home. You can keep a simple activity record and review progress at follow-up appointments. Confidence often grows from having a plan you understand and can repeat.

Do not wait for a rehab session to report worsening symptoms. Severe chest pain, fainting or severe breathing difficulty warrant calling 911.

Questions for your next visit

  • Am I eligible, and has a referral been sent?
  • What will my insurance cover?
  • What options fit my schedule and recovery goals?
