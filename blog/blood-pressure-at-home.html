Blood Pressure at Home: Make the Readings Useful

A calm, repeatable routine is more helpful than chasing a single number.

Updated September 8, 2026

Start here

A calm, repeatable routine is more helpful than chasing a single number.

Build a repeatable routine

Home BP becomes easier to interpret when the technique stays consistent: the right cuff, a supported seated position, a quiet rest, and two readings one minute apart. Keep both numbers from both readings.

Agree on frequency with your clinician. A structured series can be useful for diagnosis or a medication review; stable follow-up may need a lighter schedule. Checking repeatedly out of worry can make the process harder, so ask for a clear plan.

Use the record to guide a conversation

A pattern can tell us more than an isolated reading, but an average must not hide an urgent problem. Severe symptoms need emergency care. A persistent reading above 180 systolic or 120 diastolic needs prompt action as described in the BP guide.

Bring your readings and monitor to your next visit. Ask what changes should prompt an earlier call. The goal is information you can use together.

Questions for your next visit

  • What monitoring schedule should I follow?
  • When and how should I share my readings?
